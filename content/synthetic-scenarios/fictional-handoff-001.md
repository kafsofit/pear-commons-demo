# Fictional practice fixture: preparation and support

**Resource ID/version:** `fictional-handoff-001` / `0.1-design-fixture`  
**Review status:** **pending Alex K and practitioner/affected-community review**  
**Purpose:** interface design and workflow evaluation, **not training or crisis-response guidance**.

## Fiction and provenance declaration

This fixture was deliberately authored for the pear concept skeleton on 6 November 2026. It is not based on a real person, case, event, transcript, private reflection, or service-user record. It is a non-clinical situation: a worker wants to prepare, identify support, and notice assumptions before a constructed practice conversation.

The app embeds a summary of this fixture in `prototype/scenarios.js`. Both copies must retain the pending-review status. No endorsement by Intentional Peer Support, cited authors, practitioners, or affected communities is claimed.

## Intended exploration

Can a worker:

- name an intention, support, or boundary without supplying a case narrative?
- engage with optional questions rather than receive an assessment?
- preserve uncertainty and structural context?
- pause, decline, end, or seek human support without being steered toward disclosure?
- revise, choose not to save, deliberately save fictional data, export, or delete?

## Reflection prompts under review

1. **Notice:** What are you noticing now?
2. **Context:** What is affecting you—in the work and around it?
3. **Resources:** What support, relationships, or choices are available?
4. **Choice:** What, if anything, do you want to do next?

All prompts are optional. Blank, uncertain, unresolved, and non-disclosing responses are valid. The interface must not infer a condition, motive, risk, readiness, or recommended response.

## Bias and harm review prompts

- Which workers or communities might find these questions alienating, burdensome, culturally inappropriate, or professionally unsuitable?
- Could “resources” individualise structural problems or imply that a worker should simply become more resilient?
- Could “notice” invite unwanted monitoring, diagnosis, trauma disclosure, or compulsive self-surveillance?
- Could “choice” imply that staying, leaving, positivity, gratitude, or action is the correct outcome?
- Does the colleague-planning control make clear that no actual person has been contacted?
- What language, accessibility, pacing, and alternative-practice routes are needed?
