# pear / peel / The Commons

## Three lanes, distinct duties, explicit connections

**Status:** current ecosystem architecture direction. Only pear's non-AI concept walkthrough is implemented. This document supersedes earlier product-shape discussions.

> **Design centre:** support tailored for workers, by workers—without replacing human relationships, assessing workers, or turning other people's crises into infrastructure.

## The three lanes

### 1. pear — standalone, non-AI foundation

pear supports preparation, private reflection, navigation, closure, and worker-controlled saving, export, refusal, and deletion.

Its core works without AI, network access, or Commons participation. It may provide optional, worker-initiated access to The Commons, but private reflections never become shared material automatically.

### 2. peel — local, lightweight AI companion

peel is the optional AI harness to be developed with workers. It adapts support alongside each worker's journey, practice, and needs through explicit, inspectable choices.

It must not silently profile workers, infer vulnerability, train on private reflections, simulate a human peer, or replace human support. Workers must be able to inspect, correct, export, reset, and refuse its adaptations.

Local, lightweight operation is intended to reduce dependency and resource use. It is not, by itself, proof of privacy or safety.

### 3. The Commons — collective human layer

The Commons is the space for human discussion, peer support, and deliberately authored and reviewed practice knowledge.

Joining pear does not automatically join The Commons. Reading a resource does not disclose private reflections. Contribution is a separate, governed act with its own audience, licence, attribution, review, retention, correction, and withdrawal limits.
Peer support is a human relationship and practice. peel must not simulate it or become its substitute.

## Cross-lane rules

- No automatic movement of private material between lanes.
- No automated crisis response, worker scoring, employer dashboard, endurance optimization, or engagement maximization.
- No training on private worker or service-user material.
- No silent cloud fallback for a capability represented as local.
- No requirement to use peel or disclose publicly in The Commons in order to receive pear's core support.
- Moderation, cultural review, contribution, and peer support are labour and relationships, not free raw material for automation.
- Future real-case or direct-care proposals require separately established authority, governance, consent, evidence, and stop conditions.

## Implementation boundary

The public repository demonstrates pear only. Its fictional resource is not a functioning Commons, and no peel capability is implemented.

A first Fellowship milestone should prove one coherent worker journey and one bounded peel capability, while preserving pear's no-model pathway and The Commons' human, collective purpose.
