WORKER-CONTROLLED INFRASTRUCTURE · INTERACTIVE CONCEPT

Support for those
who care for others.

What if technology helped care workers pause, reflect, find human support, and organise—rather than making them endlessly available, measurable, and exposed?

Local-first by design. No account, model, analytics, or network connection required. Fictional material only.

THE PROBLEM

Care is becoming a data-extraction surface.

Small helplines, peer-support collectives, and civil-society organizations are being sold tools that automate intake, score workers, and learn from sensitive encounters. pear asks a different question: what support should travel with the worker?

THE TECHNICAL PROPOSITION

Restraint you can inspect.

Not a wellness chatbot. Not automated crisis response. A working demonstration of boundaries that other support technology can test and adopt.

01

No hidden extraction

The core works offline. Editing does not silently save, publish, invoke a model, or contact an employer.

Tested: no external requests
02

Agency is a feature

Pause, decline, seek human support, leave prompts blank, or continue without saving—without losing access to support.

Tested: refusal pathways
03

Honest copies and limits

Saving, exporting, and deleting are distinct actions. Tested save/delete failures produce persistent warnings; export completion remains browser-controlled.

Tested: save/delete failure disclosure

The original boundaries were challenged too.

During the original 6 November verification, temporary code mutations added automatic saving and a no-op deletion. The suite caught both: four tests failed for automatic save and two for no-op deletion. The unmodified implementation then passed the full suite. This is narrow mutation evidence, not exhaustive fault injection.
